01

The brief

Verte sells in India and worldwide, but Shopify can't take payment in anything except rupees for an Indian merchant, and the brand was paying for four separate apps to cover profit analytics, bundle promotions, GST compliance and reviews.

The goal was a commerce platform Verte owns: its own admin, its own checkout in each market's currency, GST built in, and profit numbers it can trust, without stopping sales while the move happens.

02

What I built

  • A merchant admin for products, collections, inventory, price lists, subscriptions, orders, carts, customers, discounts, reviews, GST, analytics, reports, content, email and team.
  • A token-authenticated storefront API for catalog, search, cart, checkout, customer accounts, discounts, inventory, reviews, content and event tracking.
  • Stripe Checkout with webhooks that turn a completed payment into an order.
  • GST invoicing with GSTR-1 export, a promotions engine, a reviews module with moderation, and profit analytics.
  • A Shopify sync that imports the catalogue and orders, so the move happens on real data.
  • A Docker review stack (admin, a demo storefront, seeded Postgres and Caddy behind basic auth) so the client can click through everything safely before it goes live.

03

How I did it

  1. May 2026

    Prove the checkout first

    The most urgent gap was charging in USD. While the platform was being built, the global store vertewatches.com went live on 19 May on its own Stripe checkout, with orders written back into Shopify. That proved the payment flow in production while sales carried on.

  2. May and June 2026

    Design for the swap

    The storefront calls commerce through one file, so moving a store from Shopify to Sparrow Commerce changes that file and nothing else. The platform is one Next.js app on PostgreSQL with Drizzle, with the admin and the storefront API as its two surfaces.

  3. June to August 2026

    Build the admin the client actually uses

    Every area of the admin was built against the apps it replaces, then the whole codebase was read end to end twice, in June and August, with the design, the reasoning and a setup runbook written down as it went.

  4. October 2026

    A readiness review, point by point

    Measured against the go-live checklist: 11 of 13 areas are built, including checkout, pricing and profit analytics, which are already live. Four built areas (customers, reviews, products and discounts) are waiting to go live with the move, and 2 are still being finished. The global store moves first. India follows, on Indian payment gateways.

Decision: keep selling while moving

Nothing switches off until its replacement has run in production. Orders kept flowing through Shopify while the new checkout proved itself, and each store moves on its own, with a one-change rollback.
